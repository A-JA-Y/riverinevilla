If you are stuck between a 3 BHK or 4 BHK in Bangalore, here is the short version. A well-planned 3 BHK is enough for most families of three or four, and it is the configuration this city buys, rents and resells most easily. A 4 BHK earns its extra cost in four situations: your parents live with you, two people work from home on most days, you host overnight guests often, or you want live-in help to have a room of their own. If none of those apply today, and you cannot see them applying within five years, the fourth room will mostly collect boxes.

That is the summary. The rest of this guide gives you the numbers behind it, because the real difference between 3 BHK and 4 BHK in Bangalore is not one bedroom. It is roughly Rs 1 crore, a bigger EMI, a thinner resale pool, and often a different part of the city.

Figures are indicative ranges from JLL’s 2025 Bangalore research, Sobha’s Bangalore market notes and current portal listings. Your project will differ; the gaps between the two columns are what to pay attention to.

## What a 3 BHK gives you in Bangalore

Three bedrooms is the city’s default home. Roughly every second apartment sold in Bangalore in 2025 was a 3 BHK, and 3 BHK apartments in Bangalore keep leading launches and sales in Whitefield, North Bangalore and the ORR belt.

The reason is simple. A master bedroom, a children’s room and one flexible room cover a family of four with one spare: the third room is a study on weekdays and a guest room when parents visit. Newer 3 BHK flats in Bangalore have also grown. The average apartment in the city went from about 1,480 sq ft in 2023 to about 1,660 sq ft by the end of 2024, so a 2026-launch 3 BHK at 1,700 to 2,000 sq ft is not the cramped unit of ten years ago.

Two practical advantages follow. A 3 BHK is the easiest home in Bangalore to sell, because the buyer pool is the largest. And it rents well: Rs 35,000 to 60,000 a month in mid-range areas, Rs 45,000 to 90,000 in premium ones, according to JLL.

## When a 4 BHK makes sense

4 BHK apartments in Bangalore are a smaller market, but demand for them is rising about 15% a year, and it is not hard to see who is driving it. Choose a 4 BHK if at least one of these is true:

* **Parents live with you, or will soon.** Three generations in a 3 BHK means someone shares a room permanently. In a 4 BHK everyone has a door to close.
* **Two of you work from home most days.** One home office in a 3 BHK is fine; two means one person works at the dining table.
* **You host overnight guests more than a few times a year.** A guest room that is also the study stops being either.
* **You want a room for live-in help.** Many 4 BHK flats in Bangalore include a separate utility room with an attached bathroom for this.
* **Your children are nearing their teens.** Two kids in one room works until about twelve. After that it does not.

If your honest answer to all five is “not really, but maybe someday”, the 4 BHK is a want, not a need. There is nothing wrong with wanting it. Just pay for it with that clarity, because “someday” rooms are the ones that stay empty.

## The real cost gap: purchase price, EMI, stamp duty, maintenance

This is where most of the difference between 3 BHK and 4 BHK in Bangalore actually lives.

**Purchase price.** In the same project or locality, budget Rs 1 crore or more extra for the 4 BHK. In central areas the gap is wider: a Koramangala 4 BHK runs Rs 4 to 7.5 Cr against Rs 2.2 to 3.9 Cr for a 3 BHK.

**EMI.** Every extra Rs 1 crore you borrow at 8.5% over 20 years adds roughly Rs 86,800 a month to your EMI. Run that against your take-home before the site visit, not after.

**Stamp duty and registration.** Karnataka charges about 5 to 6% stamp duty plus 1% registration on the agreement value. On a Rs 1 crore higher price, that is another Rs 6 to 7 lakh you never get back, on top of GST at 5% if the home is under construction.

**Maintenance and running costs.** Maintenance is billed per square foot, so a 2,400 sq ft 4 BHK pays 30 to 60% more than a 1,600 sq ft 3 BHK in the same complex, every month, forever. Add one more room to cool, light, clean and furnish.

Our rule of thumb: if the 4 BHK pushes your EMI past about 40% of household take-home, buy the better 3 BHK in the better location. A stretched 4 BHK in a weaker area is the most common regret we hear from buyers who upgraded too early.

## 3 BHK vs 4 BHK resale value and rental demand

For resale, the 3 BHK wins on liquidity. More buyers can afford it, so it sells faster and at a price closer to your ask. A 4 BHK takes longer to sell because the buyer pool is NRIs, senior professionals and joint families, and in a slow quarter that pool can go quiet.

That said, “slower” is not “worse”. In premium corridors such as Indiranagar, Koramangala and the North Bangalore airport belt, 4 BHKs hold value well, and the long-term shift towards bigger homes is real: Cushman & Wakefield counted high-end and luxury homes at 68% of Bangalore’s launches in the first quarter of 2026.

For rental yield, the 3 BHK is the safer bet in IT corridors. A 4 BHK rents to a thinner, more demanding tenant profile.

The honest verdict: location, developer and the price you pay decide resale far more than the bedroom count. A 3 BHK from a reputed developer near a metro line will outperform a 4 BHK at the wrong end of a half-built road.

## Check the carpet area before you check the bedroom count

A 4 BHK with four small rooms is a worse home than a 3 BHK with three generous ones. Modern high-rises in Bangalore typically deliver only 62 to 63% of super built-up area as carpet plus balcony, so a “1,400 sq ft” 3 BHK can be 880 to 960 sq ft of actual floor. Developers know buyers count bedrooms, which is why a 2,000 sq ft 4 BHK sometimes has rooms you cannot fit a queen bed and a wardrobe into.

## Where 3 BHKs and 4 BHKs are actually available in Bangalore

This is the part the comparison blogs skip, and it changes the decision.

**Central Bangalore** (Koramangala, Indiranagar, Jayanagar, HSR Layout) has little land left, so new supply is mostly compact 3 BHKs. A 4 BHK here is usually a penthouse, an older resale flat or a boutique project, all at a steep premium.

**East Bangalore** (Whitefield, Sarjapur Road, Gunjur) and **North Bangalore** (Hebbal, Yelahanka, the airport corridor) are where township-scale projects with proper 4 BHK floor plates are being launched. Anarock’s Q1 2026 data put 53% of Bangalore launches above Rs 1.5 Cr, and most of that is larger formats in these two corridors.

## Already own a 3 BHK? Whether to upgrade from 3 BHK to 4 BHK

If you are living in a 3 BHK and wondering whether to upgrade, four questions settle it faster than any blog:

1. **Which room is overloaded today?** If you cannot name it, you do not need the fourth room yet.
2. **Will that need last five years or more?** Parents moving in: yes. A toddler’s play corner: no.
3. **Does the upgrade move you to a worse commute or school run?** A bigger home that adds 40 minutes a day is a downgrade.
4. **Can you fund it without a bridge loan?** Selling and buying together means paying stamp duty, registration and brokerage twice, roughly 8 to 9% of the combined value gone in transaction costs.

Because of that fourth point, upgrade once and upgrade to the right thing. Which brings up the question most 3 BHK owners end up asking anyway.

## When the 4 BHK question becomes a villa question

Once a Bangalore budget crosses about Rs 4 to 5 crore, the 4 BHK apartment starts competing with a 4 BHK villa, and the villa vs apartment in Bangalore debate is really a trade between land and convenience.

A 4 BHK apartment gives you lift access, lock-and-leave security, lower maintenance effort and faster resale. A 4 BHK villa in Bangalore gives you your own plot, no shared walls, your own garden and car parks, and far fewer neighbours, at the cost of higher upkeep, a slower resale and a longer drive. Row villas and villaments in the city’s developing belts list in the Rs 3 to 6.5 Cr range for 2,500 to 4,800 sq ft. Independent villas on their own plots from listed developers are a different bracket again.

## A quick checklist before you decide

* Count the rooms you use daily, not the rooms you can imagine using.
* Get the RERA carpet area for both options and compare per-sq-ft carpet, not super built-up.
* Work out the full gap: price, EMI, stamp duty, maintenance, interiors.
* Keep the EMI under 40% of take-home.
* Drive the commute on a weekday evening.
* Check RERA registration and the developer’s delivery record before the bedroom count.
